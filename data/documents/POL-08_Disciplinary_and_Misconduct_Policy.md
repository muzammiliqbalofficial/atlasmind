## 1. Principle of Natural Justice & Industrial Harmony
Atlas Honda maintains an industrial environment grounded in mutual trust, discipline, and fairness. While corrective guidance is prioritized over punitive action, maintaining shop-floor safety, product integrity, and corporate ethics requires clear, lawful disciplinary procedures.

Every disciplinary proceeding must adhere rigorously to the principles of Natural Justice: right to know the accusations, reasonable opportunity to submit defense, impartial inquiry, and unbiased adjudication.

---

## 2. Categorization of Infractions

### 2.1 Minor Misconduct (Informal / Corrective Action)
Minor offenses generally do not cause direct endangerment or substantial financial loss. Examples include:
- Unexcused tardiness within the grace period threshold.
- Neglecting to wear official company ID badge or clean uniform in office areas.
- Leaving workplace or assembly station without prior permission during working hours.
- Minor lapses in 5S housekeeping standards.
- *Penalties:* First offense: Verbal warning / counselling; Second offense: Written warning letter placed in employee file for 6 months; Third offense: Final caution with forfeiture of good-conduct quarterly allowance.

### 2.2 Major Misconduct (Formal Inquiry Required)
Major offenses threaten plant safety, product quality, legal compliance, or the company's financial and moral integrity. Examples include:
- Theft, embezzlement, fraud, or dishonest misappropriation of company property or motorcycle parts.
- Willful insubordination or disobedience of lawful, reasonable orders issued by superiors.
- Habitual unauthorized absence exceeding ten (10) consecutive working days.
- Tampering with safety interlocks, bypass of LOTO systems, or intentional falsification of Quality Control inspection records.
- Striking work illegally, inciting illegal sit-ins, or impeding factory ingress/egress.
- Engaging in bribery, kickbacks, sexual harassment, or physical violence on company premises.
- Intoxication, narcotics possession, or consumption of banned substances on plant property.

## 3. Standard Disciplinary Workflow

```
[Incident / Report] 
       │
       ▼
[Preliminary Investigation (48 Hours)]
       │
       ▼
[Formal Show-Cause Notice Issued (72-Hour Response Window)]
       │
       ├─────────────────────────────────┐
       ▼                                 ▼
[Explanation Satisfactory]     [Explanation Unsatisfactory / Denied]
       │                                 │
[Matter Dropped / Warned]                ▼
                               [Independent Domestic Inquiry]
                                         │
                                         ▼
                               [Inquiry Report & Recommendation]
                                         │
                                         ▼
                               [Second Show-Cause Notice]
                                         │
                                         ▼
                               [Final Adjudication / Order]
```

### 3.1 Step 1: Preliminary Fact-Finding
Upon notification of an alleged misconduct, the Department Head and HR Industrial Relations Officer conduct a preliminary inquiry within 48 hours to determine prima facie substance.

### 3.2 Step 2: Formal Show-Cause Notice
If prima facie evidence exists, a formal **Show-Cause Notice** is served to the employee specifying the exact allegations, date, time, and breach of Standing Orders. The employee must submit a written defense within **seventy-two (72) hours** of receipt.

### 3.3 Step 3: Impartial Domestic Inquiry
- If the explanation is unsatisfactory or charges are contested, Management appoints an independent, impartial Inquiry Officer (an external legal counsel or senior uninvolved department manager).
- The employee has the statutory right to be assisted by a co-worker or registered union representative.
- All evidence must be presented openly; the employee has the full right to cross-examine management witnesses and present defense witnesses.
- Proceedings are recorded verbatim in bilingual format (English and Urdu) and signed by all attendees.

### 3.4 Step 4: Inquiry Report & Second Show-Cause Notice
The Inquiry Officer submits a detailed, reasoned report establishing findings of fact (guilty or exonerated). If guilt is established, HR issues a Second Show-Cause Notice detailing the proposed penalty and offering a final hearing with the Competent Authority.

### 3.5 Step 5: Final Punishment & Appeal Mechanism
- Penalties for proven Major Misconduct may include:
  1. Suspension without pay for a period not exceeding four (4) days.
  2. Withholding of increment or promotion for a specified duration.
  3. Reduction to a lower grade or post.
  4. Dismissal from service.
- **Right to Appeal:** The employee may file a formal appeal within fifteen (15) days of the final order to the Managing Director & CEO, whose review will be final and binding internally.
